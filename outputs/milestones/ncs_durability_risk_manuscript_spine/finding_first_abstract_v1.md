# Finding-first Abstract v1

Scientific candidate pipelines increasingly publish ranked objects against
reference databases that later change. We introduce PARC as a release-card
method for finite candidate queues under one-sided verification and ask a
different question: when will an apparently certified release stop being
durable after the reference moves? In WBM materials screening, candidate-level
energy margin and rank are weak predictors of future stable-to-unstable flips
(AUC 0.544 and 0.467), whereas t0 chemical-system margin-landscape and activity
features predict durability failure under GroupKFold by chemical system
(best ablation AUC 0.844; system model AUC 0.766). Removing the top 30% predicted
high-risk release cards reduces the kept flip rate from 22.7% to 12.7%, which is
risk triage rather than a repaired alpha certificate. PARC-A further shows that
scarce one-sided verification can create certified release in cell tracking far
more efficiently than random audit. Together, the results recast release as a
versioned, actively verified computational-science object.

## Scope Guardrail

This abstract does not claim prospective materials discovery, t1 alpha control,
or DFT v2 validation. The durability-risk result is a t0 public-label
release-card risk triage result, not a label-free deployment predictor and not a
replacement for recertification or independent DFT recomputation.
